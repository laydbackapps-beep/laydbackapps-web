    Website Builder for Contractors: Honest Picks

    Why Facebook and Instagram Aren't Enough for Your Trade Business

    Picture this: a homeowner Googles "plumber near me" after their pipes burst at 2 a.m. Your Instagram reels are fire, but your Facebook page shows up with zero details. They click away to the next guy with a real site. Happens every day.

    You don't own the algorithm. Organic reach on Facebook has tanked to under 6% for business pages, per Hootsuite's data. Instagram's no better—Meta's pushing ads hard. Customers want credibility when they search you out.

    A proper trade business website shows up in local searches, builds trust, and turns browsers into bookers. Time to level up.

    What Makes a Contractor Website Builder Actually Work for Trades

    Forget fancy sliders. Your site needs grit: big click-to-call buttons for emergencies, before/after photo galleries, license and insurance badges front and center, embedded Google reviews, service area pages for local SEO, and lightning-fast mobile speed because no one's waiting on 4G at a job site.

    It should scream "hire me" without you typing a word. Check out our guide on local SEO for contractors to nail those Google rankings.

    • One-click calls and texts
    • Photo-heavy portfolios
    • Review widgets
    • Local pages (e.g., "Plumbing in [City]")
    • Mobile-first, under 3-second loads

    Best Website Builder for Plumbers, Electricians, Roofers, and Contractors: Real Comparison

    We tested these through a roofer's eyes—no BS. Busy trades hate design time, so we eyed setup speed, trade fit, and costs. Here's the contractor website builder lineup, including the electrician website builder contenders.

    Wix: Flexible, But You'll Sink Hours In

    Drag-and-drop king, but templates aren't trade-tuned. Good for custom galleries.

    • Pros: Tons of apps for bookings/reviews
    • Cons: Learning curve eats your day; no auto-import from social

    $17/mo base + $10-20 for booking apps. Ads gone at paid tiers.

    Squarespace: Slick Looks for Construction Company Website Design

    Pretty portfolios, but creatives-first. Weak service pages hurt local SEO.

    • Pros: Mobile speed beast; built-in scheduling
    • Cons: Pricey extensions; rigid for trades tweaks

    $16/mo starts. Bookings included, but ecommerce extra.

    GoDaddy: Quick, But Templates Feel Old

    Setup in minutes via AI. Upsells everywhere.

    • Pros: Cheap entry; marketing tools
    • Cons: Dated designs; pushy add-ons

    $10/mo. Bookings $10 extra.

    Durable: AI Speed Demon, Copy's Bland

    Site in 30 seconds. Generic text doesn't match your voice.

    • Pros: Dirt fast
    • Cons: Limited edits; no trade depth

    $12/mo. Basic features included.

    Weebly: Budget Basic, Kinda Stale

    Square-owned, simple. Feels 2010s.

    • Pros: Free tier
    • Cons: Clunky mobile; few trade templates

    $10/mo pro.

    WordPress: Power User Only

    Ultimate control, but hosting, updates, plugins? Nightmare for roofers.

    • Pros: Free core; endless plugins
    • Cons: Maintenance hell; hacks common

    $5-30/mo hosting + time.

    TradeSite: Social-to-Site for Best Website Builder for Plumbers

    New kid: Links your Insta/FB, auto-pulls photos, services, voice. Trade-smart.

    • Pros: 5-min setup; trade templates
    • Cons: Early stage, fewer apps than Wix

    Tired of blank pages? Builders like TradeSite scan your Instagram or Facebook—pulls posts, detects services (e.g., HVAC installs), grabs your lingo, spits out a full site. No typing bios or uploading pics.

    TradeSite's Edge for Real Trades Work

    • Auto brand voice from social captions
    • Service detection + area pages
    • Drag-drop sections, click-to-call baked in
    • Parts/merch inventory
    • Payments setup, reviews embed
    • Limits: Less wild custom than WP, still growing apps

    Honest Picks: Which Website Builder for Contractors Fits Your Trade?

    Plumbers and electricians glued to Instagram? TradeSite—imports your hustle, done.

    Roofers/contractors with big portfolios? Squarespace or TradeSite for galleries.

    Techy HVAC guys? WordPress if you got time.

    Everyone else: Ditch the time sinks. Get a site that books jobs while you wrench.
