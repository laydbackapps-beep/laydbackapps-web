    Quoting software for tradies compared: pick by your bottleneck, not the feature list

    Quoting software for tradies compared: pick by your bottleneck, not the feature list

    Pick the quoting tool that closes the leak you actually have. If quotes leave the driveway too slowly, you need mobile quoting that builds from a price book. If they leave fast and priced wrong, you need the price book before the app. If they leave fine and die in silence, you need follow-up that runs without you remembering it. Feature lists rank tools. Bottlenecks choose them.

    The order matters because quoting is a chain, not a task. Something gets measured, something gets priced, something gets sent, something gets chased. Every quoting app on the market sells you the middle two links and leaves the ends to you, which is why two tradies can buy the same subscription and get opposite results from it.

    What is your quoting bottleneck?

    Answer one question before you open a pricing page. Where does a quote of yours actually die?

    Three answers cover nearly every trade business.

    It dies before it is written. You finish the visit, drive to the next job, and the quote waits for a night you are not wrecked. By then the customer has rung someone else. The leak is the gap between the visit and the send.

    It dies at the price. You write it, but the numbers come out of your head at 9pm and you leave money on the table or scare the job off. The leak is the price book, or the lack of one.

    It dies after it is sent. The quote goes out and nothing follows it. No reminder at three days, no call at a week, no decision recorded. The leak is follow-through, and it is the most expensive of the three because the work of quoting is already paid for.

    Write down which one it is. Everything below hangs off that answer, and a tool bought for the wrong leak will feel like a waste of money even when it works exactly as advertised.

    Do you need an app or an automation layer?

    An app is a place to write and send a quote. An automation layer is the thing that moves a quote through its stages without a person remembering to.

    If your leak is speed, an app is enough. Mobile quoting that assembles from a price book lets you send from the driveway, before you have turned the ute around. If your leak is price, an app with a real price book is enough, and the price book is the part you must build whether the software helps you or not. If your leak is follow-through, an app is not enough on its own. Most quoting apps will send one reminder at best, and only if you set it per job. A follow-up layer decides for you: day three, day seven, day fourteen, escalating in tone and copying you in.

    The test is simple. Describe your leak and ask what the tool does at that exact point. A vendor who answers with a feature name has not heard the question. A vendor who answers with a sequence of steps has.

    What should quoting software actually do for a trade business?

    Four things, and only four.

    Carry a price book. Every trade has a schedule of rates, and every quote should be built from it rather than from memory. A price book is what stops the 9pm guess, and it is the single highest value object in any quoting setup.

    Build a quote on site. Templates per job type, pick the items, adjust the quantities, done in minutes with the customer standing there. Anything that needs a laptop and a desk has already lost the speed argument.

    Send and record the send. The quote leaves by email or text with a link, and the system records when it was opened, when it was accepted and when it was declined. A quote nobody can see the status of is a quote nobody will chase.

    Produce the paperwork after acceptance. A quote that is accepted becomes a job, then an invoice. The Australian Taxation Office requires a tax invoice within 28 days of a customer asking for one, unless the sale is $82.50 or less, and a sale of $1,000 or more needs the buyer's identity or ABN and address on the invoice (ATO on tax invoices). A quoting tool that cannot hand clean data to your invoicing is a tool that makes you retype a job you have already won.

    A GST credit on what you buy for a job has a four year time limit, so the record behind a quote is still worth something long after the job is closed (ATO on claiming GST credits).

    How do the common options compare?

    Compare by class, not by brand. Four classes exist, and each one closes a different leak.

    ClassThe leak it closesWhat it costs to run each monthWhen it is the right call
    Paper and a templateNone, but it is honest about thatInk and your eveningsOne quote a week, simple repeat work, customer standing beside you
    Quoting app with a price bookSpeed, and price accuracyPer user, per month, foreverMost trade businesses. This is the class worth the money
    Quoting app plus a follow-up layerFollow-throughApp fee plus a build, or a monthly retainerAnything with a quote value over a few thousand dollars, where one recovered job pays for a year

    Two honest warnings about the top of that table. A suite sold to a two person business is a system nobody maintains, and the price book inside it still does not exist until someone writes it. A follow-up layer bolted onto a business that does not send quotes quickly just chases bad quotes faster.

    The bottom of the table is not a joke either. If you quote from a template and win most of what you send, the software is solving a problem you do not have yet.

    What does the cheapest workable setup look like?

    Cheapest workable, not cheapest. There is a difference and it is the whole point of this section.

    Start with the price book. It costs nothing but a weekend and it is the item that makes every later step cheaper. Write your rates per job type, your callout, your materials markup, and the three or four extras that always come up. Keep it somewhere the quoting tool can use it.

    Then buy the smallest app that reads a price book and sends from a phone. Not the suite. Not the one with the CRM module you will never open. The one that does the four things above and nothing else.

    Then, and only if your leak was follow-through, add the layer that chases. It can be a sequence you build once or a person who owns it daily. What it cannot be is you remembering, because you will not.

    If that reads like a build rather than a purchase, it is. The purchase is one line of it. The rest is the part that decides whether the tool earns anything.

    Questions tradies ask before switching

    Does quoting software win more jobs?

    No. Speed wins jobs, and software only makes speed repeatable. A slow operator with a good app is still slow. The app removes the friction between knowing the price and sending it, and that friction is worth real money, but it is not the app doing the winning.

    Should I switch from my current app or add to it?

    Add to it, unless the current app cannot carry a price book or send from a phone. Switching costs you the setup work and the customer history, and most switches are made for a feature the old tool also had. If the app is fine and the follow-through is not, fix the follow-through.

    Does it work with no signal on site?

    Test this before you pay. Ask for a trial on a real job in a real black spot, and check whether the quote saves locally and sends when you have bars again. Plenty of mobile tools look fine in a demo on wifi and fall over in a shed.

    Can it feed my invoicing and bookkeeping?

    Ask directly what it exports and to what. A tool that hands clean line items to your accounting package saves a rekey per job. A tool that hands you a PDF puts you back to typing. Check the connection before you commit, because adding it after a year of data is painful.
