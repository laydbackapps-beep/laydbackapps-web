How this work goes wrong

The mistakes get covered up

A wall that was not checked before it was opened, a floor that was not levelled before the tiles went down, a waterproofing membrane that was never inspected — none of it looks wrong on the day. It looks wrong a season later, when a door will not close or a shower leaks into the room below.

So the scope is written down before the job starts: what is being removed, what is being built, what is an allowance and what is fixed. If something behind the wall changes the job, that is raised when it is found rather than buried in the final invoice.

And the sequence matters. Trades stacked on top of each other is how a six-week renovation becomes a four-month one — so the order is planned around inspections and drying times, not around who is available first.

Questions

Before you book it in

Do you work from plans or just an idea?
Both. With plans, the quote is built from them. Without, the site is inspected and the scope written down so you are pricing the same job that will be built.
Do you take on small jobs?
Yes. A single room or a repair is still a job, and it is quoted the same way — scope first, price second.
Who arranges inspections and certificates?
Confirmed before work starts — which inspections apply, who books them, and what paperwork you are handed at completion.
Can you work with my own trades or designer?
Yes. If you have a designer, an engineer or trades already booked, the sequence is agreed with them before work starts rather than worked around afterwards.
How is the work priced?
By scope — labour, materials and allowances set out separately, so you can see what is driving the number rather than one lump sum.
