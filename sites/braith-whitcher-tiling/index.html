What we lay

The work, tile by tile

Most jobs come down to the same four questions: what is under the tile, where the falls go, how the set-out lands on the walls, and what finish you want to look at for the next twenty years.

01

Bathrooms & wet areas

Floor falls to the waste, waterproofing to the corners and penetrations, then wall and floor tiling set out so the cuts land in the least obvious place.

02

Kitchen splashbacks

Cut to the bench and window reveals, with the layout centred on the cooktop or the rangehood so it reads straight at eye level.

03

Floors

Screed checked for level and hollows before a bag is opened, then laid on a full bed.

04

Large format

Big tiles need flat substrate and a plan — back-buttered, lippage controlled, joints kept tight.

05

Mosaics & feature walls

Sheets set to a line, not floated — the pattern stays true across the whole wall.

06

Repairs & re-grout

Loose or drummy tiles taken up, old grout raked out, silicones replaced where they have gone black.

How a set runs

From measure to final clean

01

Measure

Rooms measured, substrate checked, falls and waste positions noted.

02

Set out

Layout marked on the floor and walls so cuts are decided before cutting.

03

Prep

Screed, sheeting and waterproofing done and left to cure properly.

04

Lay

Tiles set to line, spacers kept true, checked for lippage as it goes.

05

Grout & seal

Grout, silicone to the wet joints, floor wiped down and left clean.

Materials

What suits the room

PorcelainDense and low absorption — the default for floors and wet areas, in matt or lappato.
CeramicLighter and easier to cut, best kept to walls where there is no heavy wear.
Natural stoneTravertine, marble and bluestone — needs sealing, and the layout has to respect the veining.
MosaicSmall sheets for shower floors and feature bands, set to a line so the pattern stays true.
Underlay & screedWhere the floor is out, it gets packed or screeded first — tiles only look flat if the base is.
Questions

Before you book

If it is not here, ring the number in the header or send it through the form — you get a straight answer either way.

Do you supply the tiles or do I?
Either. Plenty of people pick their own tile and have it delivered; if you want help choosing something that suits the room, that gets sorted before the job is priced.
How is the price worked out?
Square metres, the state of the substrate, how many cuts and wet areas are involved, and whether the old surface has to come up. It is priced after the room has been looked at.
Can you tile over the existing tiles?
Sometimes — it depends on what is there and how sound it is. If it is not a good idea, you will be told why rather than finding out later.
How long does a bathroom take?
Waterproofing has to cure before tiling starts, so it is days rather than hours. The sequence gets explained up front so you know when the room is usable again.
Do you do small jobs?
Yes — a splashback, a repaired shower base or a re-grout is a normal job, not an inconvenience.
