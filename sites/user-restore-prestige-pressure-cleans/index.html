What we clean

Every surface gets its own pressure setting

Pressure is matched to the surface. Too much and pavers get stripped and render gets gouged; not enough and the grime just moves around. That setting is the whole job.

Driveways & concrete

Tyre film, oil marks and the grey haze that makes concrete look older than it is — washed back, edges and expansion joints cleaned out too.

Pavers & paths

Brick and stone pavers cleaned without blowing the joint sand out from between them, so the path looks new and still sits flat.

Render & house washing

Exterior walls washed down at low pressure with the right mix for the surface — mould, dust and spider marks off, paint left intact.

Roofs & gutters

Concrete tile and metal roofs cleaned of lichen and built-up dirt, with the gutters cleared so the wash doesn't end up in them.

Patios, decks & pool surrounds

Outdoor living areas, coping and non-slip surfaces cleaned of algae and sunscreen film — the slippery green stuff goes first.

Move-out & pre-sale

Whole-property exterior wash before photos or an inspection, so the first impression is the surface, not the staining.

Why it works

Tyre film, algae, silt in the broom

Most of what makes a driveway look tired isn't wear. It's a film sitting on top of it.

01Tyre and oil filmThe dark tracks where cars park and turn — the single biggest difference on a concrete drive.
02Organic growthAlgae, moss and lichen in the shaded spots, holding damp against the surface and making it slick.
03Dirt in the textureSilt packed into the broomed finish and joints — lifted out rather than pushed deeper in.
