01 / Services

Four jobs, done in this order.

A detail isn't one product. It's decontamination, then correction, then protection, and the last two only work if the first one was done properly.

Interior detail

Full vacuum including under-seat and boot, steam through vents and seams, fabric or leather cleaned and conditioned, glass done inside, plastics dressed matte rather than wet.

Trim, carpets, leather, vents

Exterior decontamination

Two-bucket wash, tar and iron removal, clay bar over every panel, then a rinse and dry that doesn't put the swirls straight back in. Wheels and arches done off the car.

Iron, tar, clay, wheels

Machine paint correction

Measured, then compounded and polished panel by panel under proper light. Depth of correction agreed before starting, a daily driver doesn't need the same pass as a weekend car.

Compound, polish, refine

Protection

Sealant for a straightforward top-up, or a coating for longer-term gloss and easier washing. Applied to corrected paint only, then left to cure before the car goes out.

Sealant, coating, cure

02 / Why paint looks flat

You don't see the paint. You see the swirls in it.

Under a low sun or a service-station light, micro-marring scatters the reflection instead of returning it. That is the grey haze on a clean car, not dirt, and not something a wash will fix.

03 / Process

A car is in the bay for a reason, in stages.

Stage 01

Assess

Paint depth read across panels, existing defects mapped, and the level of correction you actually want agreed before anything touches the car.

Stage 02

Decontaminate

Wash, iron and tar removal, clay. Correction done on dirty paint is correction wasted, the grit just gets dragged around.

Stage 03

Correct

Machine work in sections under inspection light, panel by panel, wiping down between passes so the finish is judged on the paint and not on polish residue.

Stage 04

Protect

Sealant or coating over corrected paint, cured indoors, with the maintenance routine explained so the finish isn't undone in the first month.

04 / Questions

Asked before every booking.

How long does a full detail take?

It depends on paint condition and whether correction is included, a maintenance detail and a two-stage correction are not the same day. The realistic time is given at assessment, after the paint has been read, rather than quoted blind over the phone.

Can you remove every scratch?

No, and any detailer who says yes is either guessing or planning to take too much clear coat. Swirls and light marring come out. Scratches you can feel with a fingernail have gone through the clear and are a paint repair, not a polish.

Is a coating worth it over a sealant?

If the car lives outside or gets washed often, a coating holds gloss longer and makes the wash quicker. If it's garaged and driven occasionally, a good sealant topped up regularly does most of the same job for less.

Do you need the car for the interior and exterior separately?

No, they're done in one booking. Interior work happens while the paint is decontaminating or curing, which is why a combined detail is more efficient than two separate visits.
