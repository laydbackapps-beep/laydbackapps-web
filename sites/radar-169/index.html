How a job runs

The detail library

Where the eye actually reads the work

Finish carpentry is judged up close. These are the junctions that get looked at every day, so they are the ones set out first.

Wall panelling

Panels are worked out from the centre of the wall so the two ends finish equal. Stiles, rails and any raised field are set to one spacing, then every joint is scribed to the wall it meets rather than assumed square.

Because the layout is marked before cutting, the panelling lands level under a room that is not — a wall that runs out by twenty millimetres over four metres still finishes with even panels top and bottom.

Doors & frames

Frames go in plumb and square, packed and fixed so the reveal is the same width all the way round. Doors are then hung on their own frame with an even gap, not trimmed to hide a frame that went in out of wind.

Hardware — hinges, handles and any concealed closer — is fitted to suit the door weight, so a solid door opens on the same line in a cold snap as it did on the day it was hung.

Skirtings, architraves & mouldings

Skirtings are run level and scribed into the floor where it dips. Internal and external corners are mitred and back-cut so they close tight, and mouldings are lined through from room to room so the profile reads at the same height as you walk the hall.

Where a new opening meets original trim, the profile is matched as closely as the section allows and the junction is detailed so the join is where it belongs — at a corner, not across a wall in plain sight.

Linings & fixout

Ceiling battens, timber linings and full fixout packages are set out to the same standard: a consistent spacing that holds across the whole surface, with the edges detailed before the face goes on.

Running the fixout as one package means the linings, trim and doors are worked out together — so the architrave meets the panel at a corner that was planned, not discovered the day it goes on.

Before you call

Questions that come up first

What kind of jobs do you take on?
Finish carpentry and full fixout packages, residential and commercial — wall panelling and wainscoting, doors and frames, skirtings, architraves and mouldings, ceiling battens and timber linings, and fireplace or feature-wall framing.
Do you work off plans or take measurements on site?
Both. On a new build the set-out comes off the plans; on a renovation or fitout the room is measured and the layout marked on site, because the wall in front of you rarely matches the drawing exactly.
Can you match trim and panelling that is already in the house?
Profiles are matched as closely as the section available allows. Where an original profile is one that is no longer made, a run can be worked to suit — that is decided after looking at the existing detail.
How do you quote a job?
After the space is seen and the detail agreed, so the quote reflects the actual set-out rather than a square-metre guess. Sizes, levels and access are all taken into account before a price is put on it.
Do you do commercial fitouts as well as homes?
Yes — residential and commercial, and the finish standard does not change between the two. Shopfitting, office fitouts and residential detail all run through the same set-out process.
When does the finish work happen in the build?
Fixout happens after the shell is closed and services are roughed in, and before painting. Getting the sequencing right is part of the job — panelling and trim want to go on when the room is dry and nothing else is going to be chased through the walls behind them.
