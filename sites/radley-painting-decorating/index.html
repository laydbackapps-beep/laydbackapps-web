Prep, in plain terms

Paint does not hide a bad surface, it magnifies it. Once a wall is coated in a low-sheen finish, every ridge of unsanded filler and every raised edge of old paint shows up in raking light — and by then the only fix is to sand back and start again.

So the preparation is done as a stage in its own right: patches filled and left to cure properly, sanded flat, dusted down, and spot-primed where the surface is bare or porous. On the outside that means treating mould and chalky paint before anything goes on, because a coating that cannot bond will fail no matter how good the product is.

Masking is part of the same stage — floors covered, fittings removed where they can be, edges taped and then checked with a torch before the first coat. It is slower than cutting in by eye, and it is the reason a finished room does not end up with a line of paint along the skirting.

Colour is checked in the room it is going in, on a patch, in the light it will actually be seen in. A colour that reads warm in the shop can read green in a north-facing hall, and it is easier to find that out on a sample board than on a whole wall.

How a quote is worked out

Four things decide the number, and all four are checked on site. No square-metre guess arrives by text.

01

Measure the area

Wall and ceiling area, ceiling height, and how many coats the surface will actually take.

02

Assess the surface

Peeling, chalky render, water stains, old gloss. Prep is usually the larger half of the job.

03

Count the detail

Windows, doors, architraves and cornice all take time per metre that walls do not.

04

Set the access

Height, scaffolding, and whether furniture has to be moved out of the way each day.

Get a quote

Send the address, what needs painting and roughly when. If you have photos of the walls, include them and the estimate gets more accurate.
