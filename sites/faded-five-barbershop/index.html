The cut list

Four things we do properly, rather than twelve we do quickly.

Every cut starts with the same question: how long before you want to be back? That answer sets the guard, the blend height and whether the top is scissor-worked or left blunt.

Skin fade

Clipper to skin at the nape and sides, graduated up to the parting. The longer you go between cuts, the higher the blend starts so it holds its shape as it grows out.

Taper & low fade

For hair that needs to keep a bit of length at the neckline, tapered at the edges only, weight left through the back so there is something to work with next visit.

Scissor cut

Cut on wet hair, checked dry. Point-cut through the ends for movement rather than a hard line, with the shape followed around the growth pattern instead of forced flat.

Beard trim & line-up

Razor edge on the cheek and neckline, length taken down evenly with the grain. The line is set to the jaw, not the hairline, so it stays straight between visits.

In the chair

Three steps, no upselling at the mirror.

Say what you normally get

Guard number if you know it, or the last cut you were happy with. If you are growing something out, say so before the clippers come out.

Cut, then check it dry

Fades are blended dry so the graduation is judged on the finished line. Top is cut last, once the sides are set.

Set the next one

If you want the same cut to hold, the next visit usually lands three to four weeks out. Book it before you leave the chair.

Questions

What people ask before the first cut.

Do I need to book, or can I walk in?

Both work. Booked chairs are held, walk-ins are fitted in around them. If your window is short, message the shop on Instagram before you leave and ask what the wait looks like.

How do I ask for a fade without the wrong guard number?

Describe where you want it to finish: skin at the nape, a shade left in the sides, or a taper that keeps length. Bring a photo of a cut you have had before, that is more accurate than any guard number.

Will a fade look grown out in two weeks?

It will soften, and that is the point of blending it rather than cutting a hard line. If you want it to still read sharp at three weeks, ask for the blend to start higher.

Can you shape a beard I am still growing?

Yes, but the line gets set conservatively, enough to tidy the cheeks and neck without cutting into coverage you still need.
