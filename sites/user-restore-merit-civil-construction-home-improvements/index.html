01 / Scope

What each stream actually covers.

Most enquiries to a builder are vague for a good reason: the work sits across two or three of these. MERIT quotes them as separate line items so you can see what the ground and the structure are each costing you.

02 / Method

How a MERIT job runs.

Five stages, in order. Nothing gets ordered or demolished before stage two is signed, because that is where the surprises get priced instead of absorbed.

  1. Site visit and measure

    Someone walks the site, checks access, levels and services, and photographs the existing condition. This is where a job either fits the drawings or doesn't.

  2. Written scope and programme

    Itemised by stage — preliminaries, ground works, structure, finishes. Assumptions and exclusions are listed separately so they can be argued with before work starts.

  3. Approvals and engineering

    Where the work needs certification, the engineering detail and any council or private certifier steps are confirmed before a start date is given.

  4. Build, in inspected stages

    Ground, frame and waterproofing stages are held for inspection rather than covered over. Progress is reported at those points, not only at the end.

  5. Handover with a defect list

    Walk the job together, write the list down, and close it out. Practical completion means the list exists and has an owner, not that the last tradie left.

04 / Questions

The four we get asked first.

Can one quote cover civil work and the build?

Yes, but they're priced as separate sections. Ground work is priced off the survey and engineering; the build is priced off the drawings. Bundling them into one number hides which part is driving the cost, which is usually the part you'd want to change.

Do you work off someone else's drawings?

Regularly — most extension and improvement work already has a designer or building designer attached. What changes is that the scope gets re-read against site conditions before pricing, so a detail that doesn't suit the ground gets flagged rather than built.

What happens if the ground isn't what the drawings assume?

Work stops at that stage and the difference is documented — depth, spoil, rock, services. You get the variation in writing with a cost before it's carried out, not a line on the final invoice explaining why the number moved.

Is a small home improvement job too small?

Depends on scope rather than size. A bathroom rebuild with waterproofing and tiling is a normal job. A single repair is better handled by a handyman, and we'll say so rather than quote for it.
