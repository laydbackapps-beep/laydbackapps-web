The material

The same wall looks different on every house

Natural stone is not a flat surface: the face, the depth and the tone change with the light. That is the reason to use it, and the reason it has to be set out properly — a course that runs out of level shows up far more in stone than it would in render.

Stone is also heavier than it looks. The wall has to be right behind it — flat, prepared and able to carry the weight — which is the part of the job that is never visible afterwards.

  • Set out first. Courses run from the corners and openings, not from wherever the first sheet lands.
  • Preparation. A flat, prepared substrate before any stone goes on it.
  • Corners. External corners mitred so the return reads as the same piece of stone.
  • Pointing and clean. Pointed flush, then washed down before handover.

Questions

Asked before most quotes

How long does a stone wall take?
A single feature wall is usually a few days. A full facade takes longer, because the coursing has to be set out around every opening and the corners dressed — the layout is most of the job on a large wall.
Can stone go over the render or brick that is already there?
Usually yes, as long as the surface is sound and flat and the wall can carry the weight. If it is not level, that gets fixed first, because the stone face follows whatever it is sitting on.
Does it need sealing?
Interior stone generally does not. Exterior stone can be sealed to slow staining and make cleaning easier, depending on the finish — it is worth discussing against how exposed the wall is.
What about the corners?
External corners are mitred so the return looks like the same piece of stone. Left open they read as a thin edge, which is the first thing that makes a stone wall look cheap.
Do you work to a builder's set-out?
Yes. On new builds the coursing, the stone selection and the junctions to other finishes are agreed with the builder before the first course goes down.
