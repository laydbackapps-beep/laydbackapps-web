What we pour

Concreters get judged on the finish and the fall. Every job below starts with levels taken off the ground and the finish agreed before the first board goes down.

01Driveways & crossovers

Excavated, formed and steel-reinforced, then poured and finished to an agreed fall so water runs away from the house and not toward it. Crossovers are formed to the right width for the driveway.

02House & shed slabs

Slab height set off the finished floor level, thickness and mesh sized to what is going on top, and the surface left flat enough to build straight off.

03Footings & set-out

Trenches dug to the plan, steel tied and checked for cover, and the set-out string-lined so the building sits square to the boundary.

04Exposed aggregate

Aggregate cast into the surface and washed back evenly while the slab is still green, with the colour and stone mix agreed on a sample panel first.

05Paths & side access

Footpaths, side paths and step treads formed to consistent widths and finished broom or aggregate to suit the wet areas around the house.

06Coloured & decorative concrete

Oxide colours, release and saw-cut patterns chosen from a physical sample before the pour, so the slab that turns up is the slab that was priced.

How a pour runs

Same four steps on a garden path and on a shed slab.

STEP 01

Set out & levels

We mark the extents on the ground, take the levels and confirm the finish and the fall with you on site before anything is cut.

STEP 02

Form & steel

Boards are set and pegged, mesh or bar tied and the subgrade compacted. Concrete is ordered once the box is square and true.

STEP 03

Pour day

The truck is met on site and the load is placed, screeded and floated. Exposed aggregate is washed back the same day, not left to the next morning.

STEP 04

Finish & cure

Control joints are cut where the slab needs them, the formwork is stripped and the site is left swept and ready to use.

Common questions

Do you quote from the site or over the phone?

From the site. The extents, access, levels and the finish are what decide the price, and guessing them over the phone is how quotes end up wrong.

How long before I can drive on a new driveway?

It stays fenced while it cures and we tell you the day you can put a car on it. Exposed aggregate and coloured slabs are usually the slowest to come up to strength.

Can you match the existing concrete?

Often, yes. Bring a photo of the existing slab and the finish and we will work the new pour to sit alongside it, joints lined up where the layout allows.

Do you do crossovers and council requirements?

Paths and driveway crossovers are part of the work. Where a crossover needs council approval we build to the approved width and levels.
