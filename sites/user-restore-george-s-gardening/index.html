The idea

Most gardens don't need a redesign. They need someone to turn up, cut it properly, and keep the edges sharp every few weeks.

That's the whole job: consistent garden care, on a routine, without a crew of six and a quote that reads like a renovation.

What I do

The work, in plain terms.

Four things, done properly. Anything beyond them gets talked about first rather than quietly added to a bill.

Mowing and edging

Cut at the right height for the grass and the season, with the edges cut in and the hard surfaces blown clean. Straight lines, no clippings left on the path.

Hedging and pruning

Hedges squared and shaped, shrubs cut back off windows, paths and gutters. Everything cut comes off site rather than into a pile behind the shed.

Beds, weeds and mulch

Beds turned over and weeded, mulch topped up where it's gone thin, and the garden kept readable instead of a scramble of growth.

Reset tidy-ups

For a garden that's got away — a full clear-out of overgrowth, then back onto a regular run so it doesn't happen again.

The routine

Four steps, and the garden looks finished when I drive off.

  1. Walk it firstA quick look around before anything gets cut, so the beds, the taps and anything fragile are known about.
  2. Cut and edgeLawn down to height, edges cut in, and the line along paths and driveway kept clean.
  3. Hedges and bedsWhatever is due on the rotation — hedges trimmed back to shape, beds weeded and tidied.
  4. Sweep up and take it awayPaths blown down, clippings and prunings loaded out. The garden should look finished when I drive off.
