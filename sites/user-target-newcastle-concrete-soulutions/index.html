How a pour runs

Five stages, one crew

Concrete is unforgiving: once it is placed, the set-out and the fall are set too. This is the order it is done in.

01

Measure & set out

Levels taken from the existing ground and the street, falls worked out, and the outline marked before anything is cut.

02

Excavate & prep

Strip to the right depth, compact the base, and get the subgrade flat — the part that decides whether a slab cracks later.

03

Form & reinforce

Formwork set to the finished level, mesh chaired up into the slab, and joints planned rather than cut wherever it decides to crack.

04

Pour & finish

Placed, screeded, floated and finished — broom, trowel or exposed — while it is still workable.

05

Cut & cure

Control cuts at the right spacing, curing and a walk-through on what to keep off it and for how long.

Questions

Asked before every quote

How long before I can drive on it?
Light foot traffic is usually fine the next day. Cars typically after seven days, and heavier vehicles longer. You get the specific timing for your pour, not a generic answer.
Will it crack?
Concrete shrinks as it cures, so control joints are cut to decide where it relieves. A properly formed, meshed and jointed slab should not develop structural cracks — surface hairline movement is normal.
Do you remove the old concrete?
Yes — breaking out and disposal is part of the quote where the old surface has to come up, and the base is re-compacted before the new slab is formed.
What finish can I have?
Broom finish is the standard non-slip surface for driveways and paths. Trowel gives a smoother look for patios and shedding, and exposed aggregate is available where specified.
