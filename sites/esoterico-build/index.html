Method

Design and building, one set of hands.

Most projects lose their intent in the gap between the drawing and the trade on site. Keeping both sides of that gap in-house is the whole point of Esoterico — and the reason the finished detail still matches what was resolved on paper.

01

The detail gets resolved early

Junctions, set-outs and services are worked through while they are still lines on a drawing — not discovered once the frames are up and the cost of changing them has multiplied.

02

One contract, start to handover

Design intent and construction sit under the same roof, so accountability does not get handed between a designer, a builder and a series of trades.

03

Performance is built in, not added

Insulation, air barrier and glazing are part of the build sequence from the first course — treated as structure, not as an upgrade signed off at the end.

Detail register

What gets drawn before it gets built.

Every project runs off a set of resolved details rather than a set of assumptions. These are the ones that decide how a house feels and performs.

01Wall & roof junctions

Where the envelope changes direction — the spot where continuity is easiest to lose and hardest to see later.

02Thermal & air barrier

Insulation runs and the airtight line, drawn as one continuous path through the section.

03Glazing & shading

Glass, frame and overhang set against orientation, so summer sun is handled by the building rather than the air-conditioning.

04Wet areas & cladding

Falls, membranes and fixing zones agreed before the first sheet goes on.
