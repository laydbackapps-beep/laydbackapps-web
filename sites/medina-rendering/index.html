Before the
first coat.

Practical answers about substrates, finish selection and repair work. The written scope confirms what your particular wall needs.

Which render system suits my walls?

That depends on the substrate, its condition and the finish you want. Medina Rendering assesses the surface first, then scopes the preparation and compatible base and finish coats.

What is the difference between cement and acrylic render?

Cement render uses a sand and cement mix and is commonly ruled before a float or sponge finish. Acrylic systems use polymer-modified products, with preparation and coats selected as a compatible system.

Can existing cracked or painted render be repaired?

It may be repairable, but the surface needs an assessment first. Loose or unsound material and the condition of any existing coating affect whether a local repair or broader preparation is appropriate.

Can a repair be blended without re-rendering everything?

A local patch can be appropriate where the surrounding render is sound. Texture, weathering and colour all affect how a repair reads, so the expected finish should be discussed before work starts.

How long between the base and finish coats?

Timing depends on the selected system, base condition, weather and the product manufacturer’s requirements. The sequence for your job is confirmed in the scope rather than treated as a fixed interval.

What helps with a quote?

Include the wall material if you know it, approximate area, photos of damage or existing texture, access details and the finish you have in mind. Those details make the first assessment more useful.
