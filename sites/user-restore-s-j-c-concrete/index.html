Concrete scope

What we pour

If it needs a level, a form and steel in it before the truck arrives, it's on this list. Anything not here, ring and ask, the answer is usually yes or a straight no, not a maybe.

House & shed slabs

Site cut, compacted base, formwork set to level and mesh placed on chairs before the pour, so the slab you get is the slab on the plan.

Driveways & crossovers

Broom-finished and jointed, with the fall running away from the garage door instead of into it. Built for a loaded trailer, not a wheelbarrow.

Exposed aggregate

Stone washed back at the right hour, not left too long and scrubbed. Even colour across the whole area, including the second truck.

Footings & pads

Strip and pad footings poured clean to depth for pergolas, sheds, air-conditioning units and bin pads.

Paths, patios & hardstand

Small pours done with the same set-out as the big ones, edged, screeded and finished so the joints line up with the house.

How a pour runs

Four stages you can see happening

No stage gets skipped because the day got long. If something changes on site, a service trench, soft ground, a late plan, you hear it from us before the concrete is ordered.

Stage 01

Quote & set out

Measure on site, confirm levels and access, then mark the extents on the ground before anything gets cut.

Stage 02

Prep & form

Strip the organics, compact the base, brace the formwork so it holds a straight edge under the pour.

Stage 03

Steel & pour

Mesh placed with cover, then concrete placed, spread and screeded while it's still workable.

Stage 04

Finish & clean up

Broom, trowel or wash-back as agreed, joints cut on time, formwork pulled and the site swept.

Ground & prep

Perth ground decides half the job

Sand is easy until it moves. The prep is where a driveway either lasts or starts cracking in the first summer, so it gets the time.

  • Compaction first, a slab on loose sand is a slab waiting to drop at one corner.
  • Drainage planned in, fall, cut-off and where the water leaves the block, decided before the pour.
  • Joints on the plan, control joints placed where the concrete wants to crack, not wherever looks neat.
  • Access checked, truck and pump reach confirmed at quote, because a barrow is not a plan.
