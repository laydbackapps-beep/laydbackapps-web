What decides the price

Tree work is priced on access and risk far more than on the size of the tree, and that is worth understanding before anyone quotes.

Access

A tree in an open back paddock comes down in sections onto the ground. The same tree between a fence, a shed and a pool has to come down in pieces, rigged and lowered — which is slower, and it is the single biggest factor in the difference between two quotes.

What is under it

Power lines, gutters, pergolas, cars and garden beds all change the method. If the drop zone is clear, the work is quicker. If it is not, the tree is climbed and lowered in sections instead of felled.

What happens to the timber

Whether the wood is left cut for firewood, taken away, or mulched on site changes both the labour and the tip fees. It is asked up front rather than assumed, because hauling a trailer of green timber is not free.

A hedge is priced on the same logic: height, how far back it is being cut, and whether the clippings are being removed. A light trim every season keeps a hedge dense; cutting hard into old wood to reduce height is a different job and can leave a hedge looking bare for a year. If a hedge has been let go, that is said before the work starts rather than discovered afterwards.

Every job gets a written scope: what is being removed or cut, how high, what is being taken away, and what the ground will look like when the crew leaves. Nothing is left as a verbal understanding, because “a bit off the top” means something different to everyone.
