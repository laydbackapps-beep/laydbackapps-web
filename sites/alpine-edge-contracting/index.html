Machinery

The gear and the operator, together

Rural work is mostly about access and ground condition. The right machine on the right job does in a day what takes a week by hand — and the wrong one makes a mess that has to be fixed afterwards.

Machinery comes with an operator, so the job is quoted on the result rather than on hire hours. That covers the odd jobs as well: a pad for a tank, a crossing that keeps washing out, a washout in a track, a heap of spoil that needs moving before it ends up in the dam.

  • Excavation — dig-outs, trenching, spoon drains and dam edges.
  • Clearing — scrub, regrowth, fallen timber and fence-line clearing.
  • Earthworks — pads, track repairs, levelling and spreading.
  • Attachments — buckets, augers and grabs matched to the job.
  • Spraying — tank and gear set up for broadacre and fence-line work.
  • Site prep — set-out, dig-out and preparation before a slab or shed goes down.

Questions

Asked before most quotes

Do you do small jobs?
Yes. A short fence repair, a washout in a track, a pad for a tank or a strainer that has pulled out are all normal work — they are quoted as their own job rather than as part of a bigger one.
Is machinery hire included with an operator?
The machine comes with an operator. That is the point of it: the job is priced on the work being done properly, not on hire hours that depend on how well the machine suits the ground.
What access do you need?
Gate width and track condition matter more than anything else. A photo of the gate and where the machine has to get to is the quickest way to answer it.
Can you work around stock?
Yes. Mob location and yard setup get worked out before the job so gates are not left open and stock are not pushed around while the machine is working.
When do you spray?
When the weed is at the right stage for it. Spraying outside that window costs the same and does less, so timing gets discussed before the tank goes out.
