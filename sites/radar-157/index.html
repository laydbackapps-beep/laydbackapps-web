What we pour

Six ways to finish a slab, and what each one asks of the prep.

The surface you pick decides the mix, the set-out and the timing of the wash. Worth settling before the pour, not after.

  • 01

    Exposed aggregate

    Stone carried in the mix and washed back once the surface has gone off, so the aggregate is the finish rather than a topping over it.

    Driveways · paths · pool surrounds
  • 02

    Coloured concrete

    Oxide pigment through the batch, so the colour is in the slab and wears with it instead of sitting on top.

    Paths · alfresco slabs · aprons
  • 03

    Decorative and sawn

    Saw cuts and pattern set out to the job, squares, diamonds or borders cut after the pour, with the joints doing the drawing.

    Driveways · courtyards · entries
  • 04

    Slabs and footings

    House slabs, shed slabs and extensions, formed, reinforced and screeded to level, with the falls set where the water has to go.

    New builds · sheds · extensions
  • 05

    Driveways

    Crossovers, aprons and full drives, formed to the line and finished so a car can use it in the first week.

    Crossovers · aprons · full drives
  • 06

How a pour runs

Four passes, and none of them can be rushed.

Set-out, steel, placement, finish. The order is the same on a garden path as it is on a house slab, and the finish is decided in the first two.

  1. 01

    Set out

    Levels, falls and the line of the slab pegged and checked against the house, the fence and the street.

  2. 02

    Form and steel

    Formwork fixed, mesh and bar tied and chaired so the steel sits in the slab, not under it.

  3. 03

    Place and screed

    Concrete placed in one run where the access allows, then screeded to the falls and edged.

  4. 04

    Finish and cure

    Wash-back, saw cuts or a trowelled surface, then left to cure before anything drives on it.

If the aggregate or the colour is the reason for the job, say so when you call. It changes the mix, the timing of the wash and how the joints are set out.
