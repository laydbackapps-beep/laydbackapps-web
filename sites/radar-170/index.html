Pour sequence

How a slab gets built

Concrete gives you one chance at the set-out, so each stage is finished before the next one starts.

01

Set out

Levels taken, fall worked out and the outline marked. Access and where the truck stands are decided now, not on the morning.

02

Prep & compact

Stripped to depth, proof-rolled and compacted in layers. Soft spots come out — a slab over a soft patch cracks whatever the mix.

03

Form & steel

Formwork to finished level, mesh and bar chaired to cover, joint and penetration positions checked before the pour.

04

Place & finish

Placed and screeded, then finished while it is still workable — pattern and aggregate worked evenly front to back.

Before you call

Common questions

What kind of concrete work do you take on?
Driveways and crossovers, house slabs and extensions, pool surrounds and alfresco areas, decorative and pattern finishes, commercial and civil slabs, and specialty slabs such as courts — residential, commercial and decorative.
How is a job priced?
After the site is looked at. Access, the base, the fall and the finish all affect the price, so a square-metre figure over the phone would not be honest.
Can you do decorative and pattern finishes?
Yes — pattern, stencil, aggregate and coloured finishes are all part of the work, worked evenly across the whole pour so the finish reads the same from one end to the other.
How long before I can use a new driveway?
Foot traffic is usually fine the next day. Cars generally wait about a week, and anything heavy longer than that. Cure times are given to you at handover rather than guessed at.
Do you work on commercial and civil jobs?
Yes. Commercial slabs and civil concrete are placed to specification, with the programme and any site requirements worked in before the pour date is set.
How soon can you get to it?
It depends on the run of jobs and the weather, which decides a lot in concreting. Call and you will be given an honest window rather than a date that slips.
