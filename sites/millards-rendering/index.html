What we do

Five ways a wall gets finished

A render job is decided before the first bag is mixed: what the wall is made of, how much sun it takes, and what the finish has to survive. That is what gets quoted.

01

Cement rendering

The traditional sand-and-cement coat, applied over brick, block or blueboard. Hard, breathable and repairable — the right choice where the wall needs to move with the weather and still take a knock.

02

Acrylic rendering

A polymer-modified render that flexes with the substrate and resists cracking better than straight cement. Used where there is movement, or where a smoother, more consistent finish is wanted.

03

Texture coating

Rolled or sprayed coatings that give the wall a fine grain, a float finish or a heavier sand texture — and put a weather skin over the render at the same time.

04

Render repairs & patching

Drummy sections cut out, edges feathered and the patch floated to match the surrounding wall. Hairline cracking raked and filled rather than painted over.

05

Restorations & recoating

Older rendered homes cleaned down, failed patches repaired and the whole wall re-coated so the finish is even from the front fence to the back corner.

Choosing a finish

Cement or acrylic, in plain terms

Both are render. The difference is how much they move and what they cost to keep. If you are not sure, the wall tells you which one it wants.

Repairs

Patch it properly, or it comes back

Most failed render starts at a crack, a downpipe or a corner that never got enough cover. Painting over it holds for a season. Cutting it back and re-rendering holds for years.

  • Drummy render sounded out and removed before patching.
  • Patches floated and textured to match the surrounding wall.
  • Cracks raked, filled and sealed rather than skimmed.
  • Re-coating only where the base render is actually sound.

Questions

Asked before every quote

How long does rendering take?
A single-storey exterior is usually a few days including preparation and curing. Two storeys, repairs or a texture coat add time — the schedule is set at quote stage, not discovered halfway through.
Can you render over existing render?
Only where the existing render is sound and properly bonded. Drummy areas are cut out first. Rendering over failed render is how a wall ends up looking worse a year later.
Which is better, cement or acrylic?
Cement is harder and more traditional; acrylic flexes more and suits newer substrates and walls with movement. The right answer depends on what the wall is built from and how much sun and weather it takes.
Do you fix cracks only, or the whole wall?
Both. Small areas can be patched and blended. Where the cracking is widespread, a full re-render or recoat usually works out cheaper than chasing patches every year.
Are you insured and do you quote in writing?
Yes — public liability cover, and every quote is written down with the scope, the finish specified and the price, so there is nothing to argue about later.
