The finish is the last few millimetres. Everything under it has to hold.

A slab is three decisions before it is a surface: how the sub-base is compacted, how the concrete is poured and edged, and how the top is finished. Only the last one shows — the other two decide whether it stays flat and drains.

  1. 01Compacted sub-base. Crushed rock, watered and rolled until it stops moving underfoot.
  2. 02Poured and edged. Slab thickness, fall away from the house, and a clean edge board line.
  3. 03Finished. Broomed, washed back, pigmented or left open-graded, then cut into panels.

Four finishes

Same crew, same base. The top is what changes.

Each finish suits a different job. Tell us the surface and how it will be used, and we will say which one fits.

  1. 01

    Plain concrete

    Broom-brushed for grip and cut into panels so it cracks where the cuts are, not across the middle.

    Suits driveways, paths, shed and carport slabs.

  2. 02

    Colour concrete

    Oxide pigment mixed through the pour, so the colour runs the full depth of the slab rather than sitting on top of it.

    Suits driveways and paths where a darker or ochre surface matches the house.

  3. 03

    Exposed aggregate

    The top of the slab is washed back once it has set, leaving the stone showing for a textured, non-slip surface.

    Suits driveways, paths and the apron around a pool.

  4. 04

    Permeable concrete

    An open-graded mix laid over a drainage bed, so water passes through the slab instead of running off to the street.

    Suits driveways and paths on tight or sloping blocks where runoff has nowhere to go.
