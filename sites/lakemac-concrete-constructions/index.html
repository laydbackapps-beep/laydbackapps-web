Method

How a pour runs

Concrete does not allow a second attempt at the set-out. Every stage below is signed off before the next one starts.

STAGE 01

Set out & levels

Benchmarks taken, falls calculated and the outline marked against the drawing or the existing ground. Access and pump position decided here, not on the morning.

STAGE 02

Excavate & compact

Strip to depth, proof-roll the subgrade and compact in layers. Soft spots get dug out — a slab over a soft patch cracks no matter how good the concrete is.

STAGE 03

Form, reinforce & inspect

Formwork to the finished level, mesh and bar chaired to cover, joints and penetrations set. Reinforcement is checked before the pour starts.

STAGE 04

Place & finish

Placed by chute or pump, vibrated, screeded and finished while it is workable. Large areas are sequenced so the finish is consistent across the whole slab.

STAGE 05

Cut, cure & hand over

Control cuts at the correct spacing, curing compound or wet cure as specified, and a walk-through with the cure times written down.

Plant & placement

One pour, not five patches

A slab poured in sections has joints where the sections meet. Where the job allows, it is placed in a single run with a pump — fewer cold joints, one finish, and a surface that reads the same across the whole area.

  • Pump placement for rear yards and tight access.
  • Pours sequenced around weather, not forced into it.
  • Formwork and steel set the day before, not the same morning.
  • Waste concrete and spoil removed from site.

Questions

Asked before every quote

Do you do commercial and civil work, or just houses?
Both. Structural slabs, footings, hardstands, kerb and crossovers run to drawings and inspection; residential driveways and paths run the same way, just smaller.
Can you get a pump into a tight rear yard?
Usually. Pump position and hose run are worked out at quote stage from the access, and if a boom cannot reach, a line pump or barrows are priced in rather than discovered on the day.
How long before we can use the slab?
Foot traffic typically the next day, vehicles after about seven days, and loaded or heavy use later than that. Cure times for your pour are given in writing at handover.
What about concrete near the lake?
Exposure matters near water — mix design, cover to reinforcement and drainage all get specified for it. Retaining and seawall work is detailed so water does not sit behind the wall.
Do you remove the old slab?
Yes. Breaking out, disposal and re-compaction of the base are part of the quote where an existing slab has to come up.
