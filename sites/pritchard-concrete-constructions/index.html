What we pour

Concrete is only as good as the preparation under it, so every job starts with the base, the fall and the steel.

Driveways & crossovers

Laid out with the fall running away from the garage, poured in one hit and finished to a surface that does not pond.

House & shed slabs

Slab set-out, vapour barrier, mesh and pour — dimensioned to the engineer's detail, not to a rule of thumb.

Exposed & honed finishes

Aggregate exposed to a consistent depth across the whole slab, so the finish reads the same at the front as it does at the back.

Paths, steps & edging

Stepped paths, garden edges and thresholds formed to shed water and take a barrow without cracking.

Retaining & sleeper walls

Concrete sleepers and poured walls with the drainage detailed behind them.

Cutting, removal & make-good

Old slabs saw-cut, broken out and carted, then made good to the new work.

How a pour runs

  1. 01

    Set out on site

    Levels taken off the actual ground, not the plan. Fall, thickness and the pour sequence are agreed before anything is ordered.

  2. 02

    Prepare and form

    Base compacted, mesh placed on chairs, formwork oiled and checked square.

  3. 03

    Pour and finish

    Poured, screeded, edged and cut on schedule; the site is swept and left ready for the next trade.
