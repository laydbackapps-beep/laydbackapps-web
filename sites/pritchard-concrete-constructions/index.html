The concrete side of a build

Whether it is a residential slab or the structure under a commercial fit-out, the work below is set out, steeled and inspected before it is poured. Concrete is the one trade with no second attempt.

01Structural

Slabs & raft slabs

Ground slabs, raft slabs and suspended pours built from the engineer's detail — with the reinforcement chaired to the specified cover rather than laid on the ground and poured over.

  • Set outDimensions and levels taken off the drawings before a peg goes in.
  • SteelMesh and bar tied, chaired to cover and inspected pre-pour.
  • FinishPower-float, trowel or broom to suit the use of the slab.
02Structural

Footings, piers & pads

Strip footings, pad footings and bored piers formed and poured to plan, with the depth confirmed against the geotech before anything is filled in — the cheapest place to get a build wrong is here.

  • DepthConfirmed on site against the engineer's specification.
  • FormworkSet, braced and checked for square and line.
  • InspectionAvailable for the certifier before the pour wherever required.
03Retaining

Retaining walls

Concrete sleeper, poured and block retaining walls detailed for the ground behind them, not just for the height in front. Drainage and backfill are part of the job, because a wall without them fails from behind.

  • DesignBuilt to the engineer's specification for the retained height.
  • DrainageAgg line, gravel and outlet behind every wall.
  • FinishOff-form or rendered face to suit the house.
04Civil & residential

Crossovers, paths & driveways

Council crossovers, paths, hardstands and driveways with the fall set to shed water to the street. Approvals are sorted before the pour so the job is not left half open waiting on paperwork.

  • ApprovalCouncil requirements for the crossover handled up front.
  • FallLevels set so water never sits against the slab or the shed.
  • FinishBroom, exposed aggregate or coloured to match the frontage.

Heavy-duty slabs, bin pads, apron slabs and hardstands for commercial and industrial sites, poured and finished to a specification that has to survive loaded vehicles rather than foot traffic.

Method

Four stages, nothing skipped

A concrete job is won or lost before the truck arrives. These are the stages a Pritchard pour moves through, in order.

STAGE 01

Set out & excavate

Levels taken off the drawing, the site stripped to depth and the base proof-rolled and compacted in layers.

STAGE 02

Form, steel & inspect

Formwork to line, reinforcement chaired to cover, embedments placed, then checked before the pour is booked.

STAGE 03

Place & finish

Concrete placed by chute or pump, vibrated and screeded, finished while it is still workable and consistent across the whole slab.

STAGE 04

Cut & cure

Sawn joints at the correct spacing and curing to specification, with the cure times written down and left with you.

Questions

Asked before a quote

Can you work from an engineer's drawings?
Yes — that is the usual way structural work is priced. Send the drawings and specification and the quote comes back against the items on them, rather than a number invented over the phone.
Do you handle council approvals for crossovers?
Crossovers over a footpath or verge need approval, and that is sorted before the pour so the job is not held up mid-way with the formwork open.
How deep should the footings be?
That is decided by the engineer and the soil on your site, not by habit. The depth is confirmed against the specification before anything is filled in, and the certifier can inspect before the pour.
Do you pour one slab in a single run?
Where access and the size of the job allow, yes. A slab poured in sections has joints where the sections meet, so a single run — with a pump if needed — gives a more consistent finish.
How soon can the slab be loaded?
Foot traffic typically the next day, vehicles around seven days, and structural or heavy loading later than that, depending on the mix and the weather. Cure times for your pour are given in writing.
Do you do residential work as well as commercial?
Both. A driveway, a shed slab or a house extension gets the same set-out and reinforcement discipline as a commercial hardstand — smaller, not looser.
