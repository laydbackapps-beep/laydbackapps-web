What gets repainted

Six jobs that make up the work. A single room and a whole house are quoted the same way — off the surfaces, not the clock.

  1. A

    Interior repaints

    Walls and ceilings through a room or the whole house, cut in by hand around the trim so the edge along the cornice and the skirting is a clean line rather than a taped guess.

  2. B

    Exterior repaints

    Render, bagged walls, weatherboards, eaves and fascia. Outside work is scheduled around the weather, because a coat that gets rained on before it cures has to come back off.

  3. C

    Feature walls

    One wall taken to a different colour or finish to break up a room, measured and masked so the neighbouring walls stay untouched.

  4. D

    Trim and doors

    Skirtings, architraves, cornices, door faces and frames sanded back, primed where the old coat has failed and finished so the brush marks do not show in the light.

  5. E

    Ceilings

    Ceilings and cornices rolled out in a flat finish, with stains and patch marks sealed first so they do not creep back through the topcoat.

  6. F

    Prep and repair

    The part nobody sees: filling, sanding, sealing and spot priming. Peeling and flaking areas are scraped back before anything goes over them.

Inside and out

The two halves of the job get treated differently, so they are quoted separately.

Interior repaints

Rooms are emptied and masked before a brush comes out, and the finish goes on in the order that keeps the lines sharp.

  • Walls, ceilings and cornices
  • Feature walls in a second colour
  • Skirtings, architraves and door faces
  • Filling, sanding and stain sealing before topcoat

Exterior repaints

Outside, the prep decides the outcome — bare and chalky surfaces are dealt with before any finish coat goes on.

  • Rendered and bagged walls
  • Weatherboards cladding and trims
  • Eaves, fascia and window frames
  • Peeling and flaking areas scraped back first

Questions we get

Can you do one room, or does it have to be the whole house?
One room is fine. A single room, a feature wall or a whole interior repaint are priced the same way — off the surfaces, the height and how much prep they need.
How much prep is involved?
As much as the surface needs. Filling, sanding and spot priming happen before any paint goes on, because a coating rolled over a bad patch shows through the finish.
Do you do exterior repaints as well as inside?
Both. Exterior repaints cover render, bagged walls, weatherboards, eaves, fascia and frames, and they are scheduled around the weather so nothing gets rained on before it cures.
Do you paint trim, doors and ceilings?
Yes — trim, doors and ceilings are normal parts of a repaint, and they are usually quoted in the same number so there is no surprise when the job starts.
How do you protect the rest of the house?
Floors, furniture and fittings are sheeted and masked before work starts, and the room is handed back cleared of drop sheets and tape when the finish is dry.
How do you quote?
From the site. The colour, the condition of the surfaces and how much repair is needed are all things you can only judge standing in the room, so the first step is a time to look.
