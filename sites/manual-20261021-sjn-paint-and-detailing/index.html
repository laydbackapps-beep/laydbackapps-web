How the work runs

Paint and detailing are two different jobs

They are often quoted together, but they follow different sequences. Knowing which one your car needs is the first conversation.

Paint work

  1. Inspect the panel and identify the existing paint system
  2. Repair and prep — fill, block, prime
  3. Colour match and spray in the booth
  4. Cut back, polish and reassemble
  5. Walk the vehicle with you in daylight before handover

Correction & protection

  1. Wash and decontaminate — clay, iron and tar removal
  2. Measure paint depth and inspect under lighting
  3. Machine polish in stages to remove defects
  4. Panel wipe and apply the coating
  5. Cure indoors, then hand over with aftercare

The workshop

Why prep decides the finish

A respray only looks as good as the panel underneath it. Filler that has not been blocked flat, primer that has not been keyed, or a blend that was rushed will show up months later as a shadow line or a wave in the reflection — long after the car has been handed back.

So the time goes into the boring part: stripping back to sound paint, repairing the damage properly, priming and blocking until the panel is straight, then matching colour against a spray-out card in daylight. Only then does the car go into the booth.

The same logic applies to correction. Paint depth is measured before a machine touches it, because polishing through a thin panel is a repaint, not a correction. If the paint cannot safely take the work, that is said before the job starts rather than discovered halfway through.

Questions

Asked before every quote

Can you match my car's existing colour?
Yes — the colour code is used, then a spray-out card is checked against the panel in daylight. Slight fade in older paint is blended rather than ignored.
Do you need the car to quote?
For paint work, yes. Damage and paint condition decide the job, and neither can be judged from a photo. Detailing can usually be quoted from photos.
What is the difference between polishing and paint correction?
Polishing brightens the surface. Correction removes the defects — swirls, sanding marks, oxidation — in measured stages, which is why it takes longer and costs more.
How long does a ceramic coating last?
Depends on the product and how the car is kept. The realistic figure for your situation is given with the quote rather than the number on the bottle.
Do you work on more than cars?
Yes — utes, 4WDs, bikes and trailers. Anything painted can be corrected and protected if the surface is sound.
