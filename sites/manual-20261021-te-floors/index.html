Floor sanding & polishing

An existing floor that looks tired usually does not need replacing — it needs sanding back and recoating. Dust-controlled belt and edge sanding, then stain, seal and top coats to bring the timber back.

  1. Check the boardsBoard thickness, previous coatings and any drum-sander damage checked before a machine goes near it.
  2. Belt sandProgressive grits across the field of the floor so the old surface comes back evenly, not in patches.
  3. Edge sandEdges, corners and doorways done with an edger so the whole floor finishes the same colour.
  4. Fill & gapNail holes and gaps filled colour-matched, then the floor is cleaned down before coating.
  5. Stain, seal & coatsStain if you want a tone change, then sealer and top coats for depth and wear protection.
  6. Polish & handoverFinal buff, furniture-safe cure time in writing, and care instructions for washing the floor.

Flooring questions

What to expect before, during and after a floor job.

Do you supply the flooring as well as install it?

Both. We can measure up and order the boards, underlay and trims for you, or lay flooring you have already purchased.

What is the difference between sanding and polishing?

Sanding removes the old surface, coatings, scratches and marks, back to bare timber. Polishing is what goes back on afterwards: stain if you want it, then seal and top coats that protect the boards.

Can you lay new flooring over the top of the old floor?

Sometimes, but usually it is a mistake. Old coverings hide uneven patches and the added height causes problems at doorways and appliances. Stripping back to the subfloor almost always gives a better result.

How long does a floor take?

A single room is usually a day. A whole house is generally two to four days depending on the subfloor, the board type and how much sanding and coating is involved.

Yes. Send through room sizes and a couple of photos for an indicative price, and we will come out and measure properly before you commit.
