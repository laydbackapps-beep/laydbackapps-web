Board schedule

Seven ways on the board

Each row is a circuit we install or replace. Most jobs touch two or three of them: a battery and a charger on the same visit, or a board upgrade that has to happen before either can go in.

Before the install

A battery is a load on the board, not a box beside it

A battery, a charger or an air conditioner is a load on the board, not a separate system bolted on beside it.

That is why the check comes first. A board with no spare ways, or with protection that no longer suits the circuits, turns a one-day install into a second visit, and sometimes into a board replacement the quote did not mention.

  • Supply

    What the property is connected to: the mains and main switch, and whether they carry the extra load without an upgrade.

  • Protection

    What each circuit sits on: whether it is RCD or RCBO protected, and what has to be replaced to suit the new work.

  • Spare ways

    How much room is left in the enclosure, and whether a larger board is the cheaper answer than a second enclosure.

The job

From the first call to switch-on

One point of contact. If the board needs work before the install, you hear it before the work is booked, not on the day.

  1. Photo of the board

    Send a photo of the open switchboard with the address and what is being added. The ways, the protection and the spare room are read off it before anyone drives out.

  2. Load and ways checked

    Supply, main switch, RCD protection and spare ways confirmed against the new load, and the scope fixed before the gear is ordered.

  3. Circuit run and wired

    The new way is run from the board, terminated, labelled and tested — solar and battery on their own protection, the charger on its own circuit.

  4. Tested, labelled, handed over

    Insulation and polarity tested, the schedule updated, and the board left labelled so the next person can read it.
