Do I need council approval for a retaining wall?

Often, yes. Anything over a metre, anything supporting a surcharge such as a driveway or a building, and anything near a boundary usually needs an engineer's design and a building approval. We tell you at the quote stage what your wall needs so you are not chasing paperwork halfway through.

What sort of wall should I build?

Concrete sleeper walls suit most residential blocks and go in quickly; core-filled block suits taller walls and tighter curves; timber is the cheapest for low garden walls but has the shortest life. The right answer depends on height, soil, access and what you intend to do above it.

Why does drainage matter so much?

Most retaining wall failures are water, not weight. An ag line, drainage aggregate and geofabric behind the wall keep the hydrostatic pressure down; without them, even a well-built wall can push out over time.

Can you get a machine into a tight backyard?

Yes — we run a skid steer for exactly that. Where access is really tight we can cart spoil by hand or use a smaller machine, and we will say up front if the access changes the price.

What about rock?

Springfield and the surrounding blocks throw a lot of rock and hard shale. If we hit rock we need to break it out, and that is quoted as a variation once you have seen it, never buried in the price.

How long does a wall take?

A typical residential sleeper wall is two to five days on site depending on length, height and access, plus the engineering lead time before we start. Walls needing approval take longer up front, so book the design early.
