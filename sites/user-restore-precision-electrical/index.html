What we do

Work starts at the board, every time

Most calls are one of these six. Each one starts the same way: find what's feeding it, prove it's dead, then rebuild it so the next person can read the board.

01 / Switchboards

Board replacements & upgrades

Old fuse boards swapped for a modern board with RCD protection, every circuit labelled and tested before the cover goes back on.

02 / Faults

Tripping, dead circuits, hot fittings

Traced with a meter rather than by swapping parts. You get told what failed and why, not just that it's working now.

03 / Power

GPOs, circuits & dedicated lines

New points, extra circuits for the shed or the oven, and dedicated lines for anything that keeps tripping a shared circuit.

04 / Lighting

Indoor, outdoor & sensor lighting

Downlights, battens, exterior and sensor lights — switching arranged so you're not walking into a dark hallway to find the second switch.

05 / Fit-outs

Renovations & small fit-outs

Rough-in through to fit-off on kitchens, extensions and shopfronts, sequenced around the other trades instead of holding them up.

06 / Testing

Safety switches & test and tag

RCD push-button testing, circuit checks and appliance testing for homes and small workplaces, with results written down.

The standard

Neat at the board, honest at the quote

Electrical work is mostly invisible once it's finished, so the only thing left to judge is how it was left: conductors dressed, terminals torqued, everything labelled.

  • 01

    Isolate, prove, then work

    Tested dead at the point of work, not just switched off at the board and assumed.

  • 02

    Terminations done properly

    Correct cable, correct breaker rating, screws torqued. Loose terminals are what turn into hot spots years later.

  • 03

    Everything labelled

    Every circuit named on the board in plain words, so the next person isn't guessing which breaker kills the shed.

  • 04

    Paperwork that matches the board

    Circuit schedule and test results written down and handed over with the job — you know what's on each breaker years later.
