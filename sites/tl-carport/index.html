Carports built for the way you park

Getting a carport right is mostly about the details before the first post goes in: how the cars sit, how much clearance the bonnet needs, where the posts can land without wrecking the driveway, and how the roof ties back into the house.

Posts and beams that do the job

We set the posts where they need to be so a car door opens and a driver does not have to squeeze past. Beams run the span the roof actually needs, and the carport is tied back into the house line properly instead of relying on the posts alone.

  • Single, double and long-run carports
  • Flat, gable and pitched roof styles
  • Timber or steel framing, your choice
  • Roof and gutter pitched away from the house

Verandahs that give the back of the house somewhere to live

A verandah changes how a house works: the back door stops being a wet walkway, the washing line and the table both get cover, and the afternoon sun stops cooking the rooms at the back.

Covered properly, from the wall out

The verandah is set out off the existing wall, with the fall built in so water runs to the gutter and not back towards the house. Posts are positioned so you keep the walkway clear, and the roof line is chosen to suit the house rather than fight it.

  • Full-width or partial verandahs
  • Roof line matched to the house
  • Posts set clear of doorways and paths
  • Ceiling and trim options discussed up front

Patios and outdoor entertaining areas

If the job is a patio rather than a verandah, the same rules apply — get the span, the fall and the posts right, and it works for years. Sort those out badly and you get a leak, a hot spot or a post in the way of the table.

Built around the yard you already have

Paving, the pool fence, the shed, the slope — we look at what is already there and set the patio out around it. If a smaller span works better than the biggest roof that fits, that is what we will quote.

Design, paperwork, then the build

Carports and verandahs usually need to be designed and drawn before anything gets built, and the requirements depend on your council, your block and how the structure sits on the boundary. We work through that with you so nothing is guessed on site.

  1. Talk it through

    Ring or send the form with the suburb and what you want covered. If you have a rough idea of size, even better.

  2. Measure on site

    We come out, look at the block, the driveway, the fall and where the posts can go, then design it to suit.

  3. Drawings & requirements

    The design is drawn up and what your council needs for the structure is sorted out before the build date.

  4. Build

    Frame, roof, flashings and gutter — with the site left tidy and a walk around with you at the end.
