Irrigation

Zoned so every corner gets water

A single sprinkler on a big yard leaves the far corners dry and the near ones soaked. Irrigation is set out in zones matched to the soil, the slope and the plants in each one, then run from a controller you can actually work.

  • Zone designLawn, beds and garden split into separate zones so each is watered for its own needs.

Install sequence

How a new lawn goes down

Turf is a living thing and it either knits into the soil in the first fortnight or it does not. The sequence below is what gives it the best chance.

DAY 1 · PREPARE

Clear, cultivate & level

Old surface removed, soil cultivated and any hard pan broken up, then levelled to a fall that drains away from the house.

DAY 2 · LAY

Roll out & join

Turf laid the day it is cut, tight joints staggered like brickwork, cut in around edges and beds, then rolled down onto the soil.

WEEKS 1–4 · ESTABLISH

Water to root down

Deep watering daily at first, tapering off as the roots knit into the soil. No mowing until it is rooted and it has lifted.

ONGOING · CARE

Mow, feed, water

First cut higher than usual and never more than a third off, then a feeding and watering routine matched to the season.

Cut at the right height for the variety, edges trimmed, hard surfaces blown clear and clippings taken away. The routine that keeps a lawn looking sharp over summer.

Questions

Asked before a quote

When is the best time to lay turf in Bundaberg?
Turf establishes best in the warmer growing months when it has time to root before any cold snap. It can be laid year-round if it is watered properly, but timing is worth getting right — it is part of what is discussed before the order goes in.
How long before a new lawn can be walked on?
Light foot traffic within the first week or two once it has knitted, but keeping off it as much as possible for the first fortnight gives the roots the best start. Once it is lifted and rooted, it takes normal use.
Do you install irrigation on a new build?
Yes — often the best time, because the zones and trenches can be set out before the turf goes down and before garden beds are planted. Irrigation and turf are planned together so the heads are not fighting the lawn layout.
How often should the lawn be mowed?
Fortnightly through the growing season and less often when growth slows. The exact routine depends on the variety and how much sun the yard gets, and it is set out after the lawn is down.
Do you take the green waste and old turf away?
Yes. Old turf, spoil and clippings leave with the crew, so the yard is left clean rather than with a pile of waste to deal with.
Can you work to a landscaping plan?
Yes. Designer plans, landscape drawings and council-approved layouts are all worked from, and the turf and irrigation are matched to what the plan already specifies.
