01 — what gets painted

Inside, outside, and the awkward parts

Repaints and single rooms, on houses that are lived in while the work happens.

Interior

Walls & ceilings

Patch, fill and sand before a coat goes near it. Low sheen on walls, flat on ceilings, so the light does not pick up every ridge.

Exterior

Weatherboard & render

Washed, scraped back where it is lifting, bare timber spot-primed, then coated in the system that surface actually needs.

Joinery

Doors, windows & trim

Deglossed and coated in enamel. Doors come off the hinges so the top and bottom edges get painted — that is where water gets in.

Details

Skirtings & architraves

Cut in by hand, filled at the mitres, sanded between coats. The lines are what people notice, not the colour.

Outside

Fences & decks

Cleaned, stripped back where the old coating has failed, and coated for the timber rather than whatever is left in the tin.

Small

Single rooms & touch-ups

One room, a hallway, a patch where something was hung. Quoted as its own job, not priced as a full house.

03 — the coats

Four coats, four jobs

Each one does something the next cannot. Skipping the sealer or the undercoat is the fastest way to a finish that looks fine for a year.

01

Sealer

Stops the surface pulling the paint in unevenly. Essential on new plaster, patch repairs and chalky render.

recoat 2–4 h
02

Primer / undercoat

Bonds to what is underneath and blocks stains and knots so they cannot bleed through the colour.

recoat 4–6 h
03

First topcoat

Builds the colour. Sanded lightly once dry so the second coat sits on a flat surface.

recoat overnight

Second topcoat

The finish coat. Full coverage, consistent sheen, no thin patches where the light hits it side-on.

04 — what goes wrong

The four failures you can see coming

None of these are paint problems. They are preparation problems that show up as paint problems.

Peeling on weatherboardsPaint applied over a damp or chalky surface lets go in sheets. It has to be scraped back to sound coating and spot primed, not painted over.
Blistering in patchesMoisture escaping from behind the film. Sealing the surface without finding where the water comes from just moves the blister.
Mould coming backMould on a ceiling or bathroom wall is a ventilation problem wearing a paint costume. Treated and coated with a mould-resistant product, or it returns by winter.
Chalking on renderPowdery render will not hold a topcoat. An alkali-resisting sealer first, otherwise the new paint dusts off within a season.
05 — colour & sheen

Two decisions, made in daylight

Colour on a wall is not colour on a chip. A sample pot painted onto the actual wall and looked at morning, midday and under the room lights will change more than any fan deck can show you.

Light reflectance matters most on a full exterior. A very dark colour on western-facing weatherboard gets hot enough to move, and a very light one shows every mark the garden throws at it.

How many colours is a decision too — one colour for walls and trim is cheaper and reads calmer; a second colour on the trim is worth it on a facade with good lines.

Sheen does the practical work. Flat hides imperfections on ceilings, low sheen is washable on walls, semi-gloss takes knocks on trim, and gloss is for doors and frames that should look sharp.

06 — how the job runs

From quote to handover

Look, then quote

Surfaces are checked before a price is given — what is failing, what needs stripping, how much prep the job really carries.

Prep

Washing, scraping, sanding, filling and masking. This is the longest part of any repaint and the part that decides how it ages.

Coats

Sealer or primer where it is needed, then topcoats at the specified recoat times — not rushed because the next job is waiting.

Handover

Masking off, furniture back, a walk-through with the owner, and the leftover paint labelled by room for touch-ups later.

07 — questions

Asked before most quotes

Can we stay in the house while it is painted?
Yes for most repaints. Rooms get worked one at a time, furniture is moved to the middle and covered, and the schedule is set so there is always somewhere to sit. Exterior work does not interrupt the house at all.
How long does a full exterior take?
A single-storey weatherboard house is usually four to seven working days depending on how much prep the boards need and, more than anything, the weather. Rain stops exterior coating and humidity slows the recoat times.
Do you fix cracks and holes first?
Yes. Filling, patching and sanding are part of the job. Structural cracking is a different thing — if a crack is moving, it gets pointed out before anything is painted over it.
What paint do you use?
Trade-grade products matched to the surface: acrylic exterior systems on weatherboard and render, enamel on trim and doors, washable low sheen on interior walls. The brand is whatever suits the job, not whatever is cheapest that week.
How soon can furniture go back?
Interior walls are touch-dry in a few hours and can take light use the next day. Enamel on doors and trim needs longer to harden — usually a week before it is safe to close doors against the frames.
Do you do small jobs?
Yes. A single room, a hallway, a front door, or the trim on one side of a house are all quoted as their own job.
