What we build

Structural and finish carpentry on the same job, so the frame, the deck and the cladding all agree with each other instead of meeting at a gap.

01

Timber framing

Walls, floors and roof framing set out from the plan, plumbed and braced. Studs at the centres the cladding and the engineer actually want.

02

Decks & pergolas

Bearers, joists and decking laid straight with consistent gaps, fixed in stainless where it counts, and posts set to a line you can see from the yard.

03

Cladding & screening

Weatherboards, battens and slat screens — set out to a module so the last board isn't a sliver, with the flashings done before the boards go on.

04

Alterations & additions

Opening up a wall, tying new work into old, matching a profile that hasn't been made since the eighties. The unglamorous half of building.

05

Finish carpentry

Doors hung true, linings and trims mitred tight, hardware sitting flush. The part everyone notices and nobody photographs.

Method

How a Kayu job runs

Same three habits on every project, whether it's a pergola or a whole frame.

Measure twice

Set-out checked against the plan and the site before cutting. A frame that starts crooked never straightens out later.

Fix it right

Correct fixings and hardware for the exposure — timber moves, and joins that don't allow for it open up inside a season.

Leave it clean

Offcuts stacked, site swept, timber protected from the weather. You should be able to use the space the day we finish.

Detail

Timber is unforgiving. It shows every out-of-square cut, every fixing driven at an angle, every join that was rushed.

That's the whole reason to care about it. The details that get covered up later — the plates, the noggins, the flashings behind the cladding — are the ones that decide how the job lasts.

We'd rather take the extra hour on the set-out than explain a cracking joint next winter.

  • Plans first. Send drawings or a sketch — scope and a realistic start date come back the same way.
  • Straight answers. If a detail won't work in timber, you'll hear it before it's built, not after.
  • One crew. Frame, deck and finish from the same hands, so nobody blames the last trade.
  • Site respect. Neighbours informed, access kept clear, mess contained.
