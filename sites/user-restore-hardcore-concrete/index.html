Scope of works

What we pour, and how it's built

Every job runs off the same sequence: set out to the plan, form it true, place the steel before a single barrow goes down, then finish and cure it properly instead of walking away at the truck.

House & shed slabs

Waffle and conventional slabs, edged, levelled and screeded to the set-out — ready for your builder, not for a phone call about a hump in the middle.

Footings & pads

Trench and pad footings dug to depth, reinforced and poured clean, so the next trade isn't chipping concrete out of a corner.

Driveways & crossovers

Control-jointed, broomed for grip and sloped away from the garage. Built to take a loaded ute in July, not just a hose.

Exposed aggregate & finishes

Aggregate washed back at the right time, sealed and consistent across the whole pour — not patchy where the second truck landed.

Paths, pads & hardstand

Pump pads, bin pads, service hardstand and garden paths: small pours done with the same set-out as the big ones.

Hardcore Concrete · On site

From string line to sawn joint

You should be able to walk out mid-job and read exactly where it's up to.

Stage 01

Set out

Levels taken, string lines run, corners squared off the plan before anything is cut or dug.

Stage 02

Form & steel

Formwork braced so it doesn't belly under the pour; mesh and bar placed with the cover it needs.

Stage 03

Pour

Placed, vibrated and screeded in one run where the access allows — one pour, one finish.

Stage 04

Finish & cure

Trowel, broom or wash-back, joints cut on time, edges detailed, site left broom clean.

Finishes

Trowel, broom, aggregate, saw cut

  • Steel trowel — hard, tight and easy to sweep. The default for sheds and garages.
  • Broom finish — the grippy one. Driveways, ramps and anything that gets wet.
  • Exposed aggregate — stone washed back for texture that hides tyre marks and dust.
  • Sawn joints — cut on schedule so the crack you were going to get happens where you planned it.

Concrete colour and aggregate vary with the mix and the pour — finishes are confirmed against a sample before we place.
