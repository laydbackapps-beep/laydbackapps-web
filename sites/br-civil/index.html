How a job runs

Four steps, in this order. The quote is the third one, because a price before a site visit is a guess.

  1. CH 0+000

    Send the plans

    A PDF, a sketch, or photos of the block with the boundaries marked. An engineer's drawing saves a trip.

  2. CH 0+120

    Site visit & levels

    Walk the block: machine access, existing services, where the spoil can go and how far the truck has to travel.

  3. CH 0+240

    Program & price

    One price for the scope, with what's included and what isn't: tip fees, unexpected rock, extra cart away, written down.

  4. CH 0+360

    Works & handover

    On site when we said, levels checked as the cut goes, batters trimmed and the block left tidy for the next trade.

Sheet S-03

What moves the price around here

Bendigo ground isn't uniform. Goldfields shale and clay sit under most of the city, rock comes up shallower on the ridges than it does on the flats, and old workings aren't always on a plan.

None of that is a surprise to a machine that has been digging here for fourteen years, but it is the reason one civil quote differs from another, so it's worth saying out loud before you compare them.

  • Rock depthWhere the shale starts decides whether it's a bucket or a hammer, and how long the cut takes.
  • AccessA 3-tonne machine through a 2.4m gate is a different job, and a slower one, than a 13-tonne on an open lot.
  • SpoilClean fill stays on site; anything else gets carted. Truck distance is a real line item, not padding.
  • ServicesUnmarked lines under a driveway turn half a day into a week. Dial-before-you-dig is part of the first visit.
