01 — what we paint

Surfaces, and what each one needs

Every surface on a coastal home behaves differently. Weatherboards swell and shrink, render holds salt, timber trim dries out. The coating is chosen for the surface, not the other way round.

01

Interior walls & ceilings

Patch, sand, prime the repairs, then two coats in the sheen that suits the room. Cut lines taped and checked under light, not eyeballed.

02

Trim, doors & architraves

Hand-prepared and finished in semi-gloss or gloss, or sprayed for a flat, even film with no brush marks on doors and joinery.

03

Weatherboards & timber

Scrape, sand, treat any mould or rot, spot-prime bare timber, then coat. On the coast this is the difference between four years and ten.

04

Render & masonry

Pressure clean, let it dry properly, and use a coating that lets moisture out — sealing damp render in is how bubbling starts.

05

Eaves, fascia & gutters

The areas nobody looks at until they fail. Prepped and coated with exterior-grade product resistant to salt and UV.

06

Commercial & strata

Programmed work around trading hours, with colour schedules and access planned before the first coat goes on.

02 — the finish

Sheen is a decision, not a default

Most repaints go wrong at the sheen choice: too flat in a hallway shows every mark, too glossy on a ceiling shows every wave. This is how it's chosen on our jobs.

03 — what goes on the wall

The coating build-up, layer by layer

A finish that lasts is a stack, not a colour. Each layer has one job, and skipping any of them is what a cheap quote is actually buying.

  1. SubstrateSound timber, render or masonry. Anything loose, chalky or damp stops the stack working from the first layer.
  2. PrimerBonds to the surface and blocks absorbency, so the topcoats sit on top instead of soaking in unevenly.
  3. UndercoatBuilds film and gives the topcoat an even tonal base — the layer that decides whether two coats actually cover.
  4. Topcoat × 2The colour and the protection. Two full coats at the specified spread rate, not one thick one.
04 — prep standard

What happens before the first coat

Prep is the part you cannot see in a finished photo and the only part that decides how long the job lasts. This is the sequence on every exterior.

07 — questions

Asked before most quotes

How long does an exterior repaint take?
A single-storey weatherboard home is usually five to eight working days once prep is counted — prep is the larger share. Rendering and two-storey work take longer, and rain delays a coastal schedule, so the window is confirmed in writing rather than promised on the phone.
Do you fix the surface or just paint over it?
Fix it. Loose paint is taken back, rot is treated or replaced, gaps are sealed and bare timber is primed. Quoting over a failing surface is how a repaint ends up costing more two years later.
What paint do you use?
Trade-range exterior and interior products from the major brands, chosen to suit the substrate and the exposure. If you have a product you want used, that is fine — it is priced into the quote.
Can you work around us living there?
Yes, one area at a time and furniture moved or covered as agreed. Interiors are scheduled so rooms come back into use as they finish.
Do you paint commercial and strata?
Yes — programmed work around trading hours, with colour schedules and access agreed before starting.
Are you insured?
Fully insured with public liability cover, and trade qualified. Certificates can be provided with the quote.
