What the workshop does

Structural steel, fabricated and installed

Four kinds of work come through the shop: steel being cut and built to a drawing, joints being welded, the finished steel being installed, and the lintels and T-bars that carry an opening.

01

Structural steel fabrication

Members, sections, plates and cleats cut, set out and assembled in the workshop at Campbellfield. The steel is squared and marked to the drawing before any arc is struck, holes are drilled and cleats positioned off the same line, and members are built so the pieces line up when they meet on site rather than being fettled into place.

  • Members & sections
  • Plates & cleats
  • Set out to drawing
02

Welding

Bead runs, tacks, stitch welds and full runs on steel section and plate. Tacks hold a joint true and square while the seam is laid in, the run is carried along the joint line at a consistent pace, and the bead is dressed and cleaned back so the finished weld sits proud of the plate without spatter or slug left around it.

  • Bead runs
  • Tacks & stitch welds
  • Dressed & cleaned
03

Steel installation

Once the fabricated steel leaves the shop it is installed — columns stood and plumbed, beams landed and fixed, and connections bolted or welded up on site. Installation is done by the same business that fabricated the steel, so anything that needs adjusting is dealt with on the spot instead of going back to a workshop.

  • Columns & beams
  • Connections fixed
  • Fabricate & fit
04

Lintels & T-bars

Lintels and T-bars fabricated for structural openings: measured off the opening, cut and welded to suit the span, with the bearing ends finished so they sit flat on the supporting work. Lintels are built as one piece unless the opening calls for something else, so they arrive ready to be lifted into place.

  • Lintels
  • T-bars
  • Structural openings

Method

How a joint is worked

A steel joint does not allow a second attempt at the set-out. The three steps below happen in order, every time, whether it is a lintel or a beam connection.

Step 01

Square and mark

Plate and section are squared, marked and set to the joint line before any arc is struck — the piece laid out so the weld goes where the drawing says it does.

Step 02

Tack and align

Tacks hold the joint true and square while the run is laid along the seam, and the alignment is checked again before the full run goes in so nothing pulls out of square as it cools.

Step 03

Run and dress

The bead is run along the joint, then dressed and cleaned back to the plate — spatter knocked off, edges tidied, and the joint left ready for the next process or a coat of paint.

On site

Steel in place

Fabricated members landed and fixed, steel frames stood and braced, and connection work photographed as it was done.

About

Steel built to the drawing, not to the gap

Vic Welding Pty Ltd is a structural steel fabrication and installation business working out of Campbellfield, VIC 3061. Work comes in as a drawing, a detail or a steel schedule: members cut and assembled in the workshop, welded at the joints, and then installed by the same crew once the site is ready for them.

Most of the steel that goes through the shop ends up in the frame of a building — beams over an opening, columns in a wall, lintels and T-bars carrying brickwork, and the braces that hold a portal frame square. Because the fabrication and the installation sit with one business, the measurements taken on site are the measurements the steel is built to.

Jobs are quoted on the steel, the drawing and the access — what the section actually is, how the joint has to be welded, and how the piece gets into place once it is made. You get a clear picture of the fabrication and the install before the steel is cut.

Questions

Asked before every quote

Do you fabricate from a drawing or do you measure the job?
Both. If there is a drawing or a steel schedule the steel is fabricated to it; if not, the opening or the frame is measured on site and the member is built to those measurements.
Can you make up lintels and T-bars to a specific opening?
Yes. Lintels and T-bars are fabricated for structural openings, built to the span and bearing that the opening needs, with the ends finished to sit flat on the supporting work.
Do you install the steel as well as make it?
Yes. Fabrication happens in the workshop at Campbellfield and installation is done by the same business, so anything needing adjustment on site is dealt with on the spot.
What do you need to quote a job?
The section sizes or a drawing, roughly how many pieces, and where the work is. A photo of the opening or the frame helps if the job is a one-off.
