Prep decides the finish

Skipping prep is what makes a repaint peel in two years. Every job runs the same five steps, in the same order, with the prep written into the price rather than added later.

STEP 01

Wash down

Mould, chalk and loose coats washed off so the new paint has something to hold.

STEP 02

Scrape & fill

Gaps, dents and bare timber filled and faired back.

STEP 03

Sand & seal

Sanded, dusted down and sealed, with stain blocking where it needs it.

STEP 04

Prime & two coats

Spot-primed, then two full coats — not a thin single pass.

STEP 05

Cut in & clean down

Masking off, edges cut in sharp, floors swept and everything put back.

Before you book

The questions that come up most, answered straight.

How is a repaint priced?

By the job, measured room by room and wall by wall, with the prep written into the price rather than added later. Interior repaints are usually quoted per room plus ceilings and trim; exteriors on the wall area, the height and how much sanding and filling the weatherboards need.

How much prep is actually included?

Sanding, filling, gap sealing, stain blocking and a proper wash-down before any paint goes on — that is the part that decides whether the finish lasts. If a surface needs more than that, such as rotting trim or peeling render, it is quoted as its own line so you can see the cost.

Do you do both interiors and exteriors?

Both. Interior walls, ceilings, trim, doors and feature walls, and exterior weatherboards, render, eaves, fascia and windows — including the masking and protection that keeps paint off everything it should not touch.

How long does a house repaint take?

A single room is a day or two; a full interior is usually one to two weeks depending on the number of rooms and how much filling is needed; an exterior runs a week or more depending on weather and access. You get a program with the quote.

Do I need to move everything out?

No. Furniture is moved to the middle of the room and covered, floors are protected, and everything goes back at the end of each day. Delicate items and wall hangings are best taken down before work starts.
