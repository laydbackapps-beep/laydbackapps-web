Scope of works

Five surfaces, one sequence

Bitumen work is mostly preparation and timing. Sweep it clean, prime what's porous, spray at the right rate, then cover and roll before the binder goes cold. These are the jobs that sequence suits.

Driveway seals & resealsResidential

A tired gravel or failing seal gets swept, patched where it's breaking up, then sprayed and covered. The result is a dark, bound surface that stops the gravel migrating to your garage door.

Car parks & parking baysCommercial

Whole-area spray sealing with the edges cut in by hand, so bays and turning circles read as one surface rather than a patchwork of separate pours.

Access roads & hardstandStrata & industrial

Long runs sprayed in continuous passes at a consistent rate, the section that matters on a shared driveway where one thin strip would ravel first.

Tack & primer coatsBefore asphalt

The coat that makes new asphalt stick to what's underneath. Sprayed thin and even, so the paving crew isn't laying over dust that lets the mat slide.

Prep, patching & edgesGroundwork

Blowing off, cutting back broken edges, filling depressions and trimming the seal line flush to kerb, garden bed or concrete apron.

The seal, in section

What's under the black

A seal is only as good as the layer below it. This is the stack a driveway reseal is built on, and the reason a seal sprayed onto wet or loose base fails in the first summer.

Compacted baseCrushed rock, shaped and rolled
PrimerSoaks in, keys the surface
Bitumen binderSprayed hot, at a set rate
AggregateSpread, then rolled in
  • Rate matters more than colourSpray too light and the stone strips out under turning tyres. Too heavy and the binder bleeds to the surface and sticks to shoes.
  • Moisture is the enemyBinder won't key to a damp base. If the ground's wet, the job waits, a seal sprayed anyway is money thrown at a surface that will lift.
  • Edges fail firstLoose gravel at the perimeter is where a seal starts unraveling, so edges get cut back and sealed by hand rather than left to the spray bar.

How it runs

Quote, prep, spray, cure

Step 01

Look and quote

Address, approximate area and a couple of photos. Surface type and condition decide the coats, and the quote follows from that.

Step 02

Prepare the base

Blown clean, edges cut back, hollows filled. Nothing gets sprayed until the base is dry and firm underfoot.

Step 03

Spray and cover

Binder sprayed at the agreed rate, aggregate spread immediately behind it, then rolled so the stone locks in.

Step 04

Keep off it

Loose stone sweeps off in the first week or two. Foot traffic and turning wheels are held back until the binder has taken.
