Pour sequence

How the week runs

Concrete does not allow a second attempt at the set-out, so each stage is finished before the next one is started.

STAGE 01

Set out

Levels taken, falls worked out and the outline marked. Access and where the truck will stand decided now, not on the morning.

STAGE 02

Excavate & compact

Strip to depth, proof-roll and compact the subgrade in layers. Soft spots are dug out, because a slab over a soft patch cracks whatever the mix.

STAGE 03

Form & steel

Formwork to the finished level, mesh and bar chaired to cover, penetration and joint positions set and checked before the pour.

STAGE 04

Place & finish

Placed by chute or pump, vibrated and screeded, then finished while it is still workable. Larger areas are run in one spread for a consistent surface.

STAGE 05

Cut & cure

Control cuts at the right spacing, curing compound or wet cure as specified, and cure times left with you in writing at handover.

Questions

Asked before most quotes

How long before I can drive on a new driveway?
Foot traffic is usually fine the next day. Cars generally wait about seven days, and anything heavy — trucks, caravans, loaded trailers — longer than that. Your cure times are written down at handover rather than guessed at.
Can you match the concrete I already have?
Colour and texture are matched as closely as the age of the existing pour allows. Older concrete weathers, so it is judged on site before quoting rather than promised over the phone.
Do you take the old slab and spoil away?
Yes. Breaking out, disposal and re-compaction of the base under the new slab are all part of the quote where an existing surface has to come up.
Will the slab crack?
All concrete moves, so the aim is to control where it cracks. That means a properly compacted base, steel chaired to cover, and saw-cut joints at the correct spacing so any movement follows the lines you can live with.
How soon can you get to it?
It depends on the run of jobs and the weather, which decides everything in concreting. Call and you will be given an honest window rather than a date that slips.
Do you do shed floors and footings, or just driveways?
All of it. Driveways, paths, shed floors, house slabs and footings — thirty years of the same work around the same towns.
