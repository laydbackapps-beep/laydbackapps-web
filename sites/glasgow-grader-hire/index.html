Final trim is a finishing trade dressed up as earthmoving. The machine is capable of moving a lot of dirt, but the job is judged on the last 100mm, whether the level holds across the whole pad, whether the falls run the way the plan says, and whether the edge is straight enough to form against.

That is why this is run as an owner-operated hire. The person who quotes the job is the person in the seat, so the level that gets discussed on the phone is the level that gets cut.

Bulk earthworks and final trim are quoted as separate scope, because they are separate skills. If the pad is still metres out, that is a bulk cut, and it is worth doing before the trim pass rather than during it.

Questions

What to sort out before the grader arrives.

What counts as final trim?

The last grading pass before concrete, paving or turf. Levels are already close; the trim brings them onto the design line, squares the edges and dresses the surface so the next trade starts on a flat, even pad.

Do I need levels set before you get there?

It helps. A plan level, laser datum or pegs already on site all work, say which one you have when you send the details, and it gets confirmed before the machine is booked.

How much notice do you need?

Message the page with the site address and the date you need the pad finished by. Availability is confirmed against the work already booked, rather than quoted blind.

Can you take on the bulk cut as well?

Ask when you send the details. Bulk earthworks and final trim are priced as separate scope so the trim rate is not carrying excavation you did not need.
