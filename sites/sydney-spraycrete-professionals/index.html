Six ways to put a new surface on old concrete

Spraycrete work is sprayed, patterned and sealed — so the finish, the colour and the grip are built into the top coat instead of sitting on the surface as paint.

01

Spray-on resurfacing

A new textured wearing coat sprayed over sound existing concrete. No demolition, no skip, no broken-up driveway sitting there for a week.

02

Stencil & pattern finishes

Flagstone, brick and slate layouts stencilled into the surface, with borders and banding worked around the house and the edges.

03

Exposed aggregate

Aggregate seeded into the top and washed back, leaving a gritty stone surface that stands up to tyres, gates and sun.

04

Driveway restoration

Faded, chalky and oil-marked driveways brought back to a sealed, even colour without lifting the slab.

05

Pool surrounds & coping

Grip-positive finishes across the pool surround and walk-on edges — a cooler surface underfoot than bare grey concrete.

06

Non-slip finishes

Textured spray coats for ramps, steps, entries, garages and any wet area where the surface has to be walked on with grip.

Worn out vs resurfaced

The old slab stays. The surface on top of it changes.

Resurfacing strips nothing out: the existing concrete is cleaned and prepped, then a new sprayed, textured and sealed coat goes down over it. What you get back is a surface with colour, pattern and grip — not a patched slab with a story.

Worn Faded grey, chalky underfoot, hairline cracking, oil marks that never come out.
Resurfaced A bonded spray coat with the colour, the stencil pattern and the non-slip texture sprayed in.

Questions

Spraycrete, answered

What actually is spraycrete?

Spraycrete is a sprayed concrete resurfacing system. The existing concrete is cleaned and prepped, then a new textured wearing coat — carrying the colour, the pattern and the non-slip grit — is sprayed over the top and sealed.

Can it go over the concrete I already have?

Resurfacing is made for existing concrete: driveways, paths, courtyards, pool surrounds and garage floors. Where the slab has failed or moved, that gets raised on the call and sorted out before any spraying is booked.

Does the old surface have to be jackhammered out?

No. The whole point of resurfacing is that the slab stays where it is — no demolition, no skip, no driveway missing for a fortnight.

Will the new surface be slippery when it's wet?

Non-slip textured finishes are part of the range, and they're what goes on ramps, steps, entries and the walk-on edges around a pool.
